### **Chapter 11. Investigate historical mirrors**

This deserves its own chapter because it is a particularly powerful and somewhat specialised research technique.

Explain how popular Quake websites were frequently mirrored across multiple servers.

A project might originally have been hosted on PlanetQuake, but copies of its website or downloads might survive on other servers.

Teach the learner to investigate:

- Archived copies of original project websites.
- PlanetQuake mirrors.
- FTP mirrors.
- Gamers.org directory structures.
- idgames archives.
- Other historical software mirrors.
- Alternative hostnames and directory paths.

For example, given a historical project hosted at `qmatrix`, researchers might investigate variations such as:

```
planetquake.com/qmatrix
planetquake.gamespy.com/qmatrix
hosted.planetquake.gamespy.com/qmatrix
qmatrix.planetquake.gamespy.com
dynamic.gamespy.com/qmatrix
dynamic0.gamespy.com/~qmatrix
dynamic1.gamespy.com/~qmatrix
```

Similarly, PlanetQuake content was mirrored across numerous FTP servers and public archives.

The guide could include a reference list of known historical mirror locations and directory structures.

I would put your extensive PlanetQuake mirror list into a downloadable reference resource or appendix rather than placing the entire list in the main chapter.

Teach the learner how to use known mirror structures as search patterns and how to identify additional mirrors from surviving directory listings.

**Outcome:** The learner can systematically investigate alternative locations where original websites and files might survive.
