# **Introduction**

more personal intro to the book by jason brownlee, almost a preface

this is not the one or best way to be a quake archivist, to archive old quake stuff, but it the way i do it and recommend to do it based no archiving for 5+ years and being very entheastic about it. all my learnings and advice togehter

also, by writing it all out i can tighten my own future archive projects.
i found many files for my archives will writing this guide becuase it reminded me of places and ways to look
this guide is valuable for that alone
also, you may decide to get started as an archivist or as an archivist you find helpful advice/tips/resources/etc

why do I it? why do i create archives
- fun
- exciting
- like a treasure hunt
- give back to the community i love
- nostelga for mid to late 1990s when i was in high school, quake 1, 2, 3 has a massive impact

why else
- am good at it
- i've been doing it for 5+ years
- i have many quake 1, 2, 3 archives
- some are widly used/cited, e..g.
	- quake official releases
		https://github.com/Jason2Brownlee/QuakeOfficialArchive
		https://github.com/Jason2Brownlee/Quake2OfficialArchive
		https://github.com/Jason2Brownlee/Quake3OfficialArchive
	- quake bot archives
		https://github.com/Jason2Brownlee/QuakeBotArchive
		https://github.com/Jason2Brownlee/Quake2BotArchive
	- and archives that capture unreleased material, like unreleased team fortress quake code bases
		https://github.com/Jason2Brownlee/TeamFortressQuakeArchive
- this gives me lots of experience
	- help you save time and be more productive
- i have created large number of archives, some deep archives, some one-off lists of releases/historical urls on demand for people that reach out and make requests

why write this book?
- improve my process by documenting it and thinking hard about it
	leads to better archives
- asked a number of times how to create archives, how to search
- give back, perhaps useful to other quake archivists and other retro game archivists generally


who is it for
- people who want to create an archive of quake materials
	 - how and why to do it
- existing quake archivists
	how to do it differently, better, share methods
- ideally people in IT and programmers
	helpful to know a lot about files, internet, programs, etc.
	most people that played these games ended up in IT, it was what we did

i sometimes wish i could go back to 1996, 1998 or 1999 and tell myself to back this or that onto cds
to have a complete record of this or that mod, website, etc.
that is the dream
everything we do is an imperfect version of that
it's frustrating, exciting, a treasure hunt

write with ai
i wrote notes on everything i could think of based on years of experience creating and maintaining archives
ai helped me shape it into chapters and then i heavily edited/refined based on what i think is most helpful
if that bothers you, i understand
maybe this guide is not for you, or maybe you can dip in and pull out a resource that will help or a techniaue that will give you ideas
