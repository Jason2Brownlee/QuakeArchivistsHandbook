# Acknowledgements

This book would not have been possible without the Quake community.

More than thirty years after the original game was released, there are still people playing Quake, creating modifications, maintaining websites, preserving old files, and sharing their knowledge with others.

I'm continually amazed by the dedication and generosity of this community.

I'd like to thank everyone who has helped me with my own Quake archival projects over the years. Many people have shared rare files, answered questions, provided historical information, corrected mistakes, and pointed me towards resources I would never have discovered on my own.

I've acknowledged all of these individuals in the archives they helped me create, and I'm grateful for every contribution.

I'd also like to offer a special thanks to the people who have spent years maintaining the websites and file archives that make Quake preservation possible.

In particular:

* **Frans P. de Vries** and all those other volunteers and stewards who maintain and keep the [gamers.org](https://gamers.org) (`https://gamers.org`) archive alive.
* **Hannes, better known as "Spirit",** for his work on [Quaddicted](https://www.quaddicted.com) (`https://www.quaddicted.com`), an extraordinary resource for Quake maps, files, and community history.
* **John Fitzgibbons, better known as "metlslime",** for creating and maintaining the [func_messageboard forum](https://celephais.net/board/) (`https://celephais.net/board/`), an important home for Quake community discussion, and [Quaketastic](https://www.quaketastic.com) (`https://www.quaketastic.com`), a valuable archive of community files.
* **Brewster Kahle and Bruce Gilliat** and the whole team behind the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org) (`https://archive.org`). What an incredible contribution to humanity!

These resources have been invaluable to my own research, and I suspect countless Quake players and archivists would say the same.

My thanks also go to the people behind the many other Quake websites, forums, file mirrors, FTP servers, and archives, both past and present. There are far too many to name here, but their collective efforts have preserved an extraordinary amount of Quake history that might otherwise have disappeared.

Finally, I'd like to thank everyone who has contacted me over the years to ask about creating their own Quake archives, request help finding missing files, or share their own archival discoveries.

Those conversations helped motivate me to turn my scattered notes, research methods, and collection of Python scripts into this book.

I hope it encourages more people to join the effort.

**Thank you to everyone who continues to keep Quake, its history, and its community alive.**
