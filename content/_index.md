# **The Quake Archivist's Handbook**

Welcome! You have discovered:

* **The Quake Archivist's Handbook:** _How to Find Lost Mods, Recover Forgotten Files, and Preserve Quake History_

Written by Jason Brownlee in November 2026.

## **Synopsis**

**Somewhere out there, a 30-year-old Quake ZIP file is waiting to be discovered.**

Maybe it's buried on a forgotten FTP mirror. Hidden inside an old magazine CD. Sitting on a retired developer's hard drive. Or preserved in a corner of the internet that Google has never indexed.

**Finding it is a treasure hunt. And this book shows you how.**

In the 1990s, Quake 1, Quake 2 and Quake 3 inspired an explosion of creativity. Thousands of mods, bots, maps, total conversions and utilities were created and shared by enthusiastic developers around the world.

Then the websites disappeared. FTP servers shut down. Download links broke. Entire projects were lost to time.

But not everything is gone.

**How to Archive Quake Mods** is a practical guide to tracking down, recovering, documenting and preserving these forgotten pieces of gaming history.

Drawing on more than five years of hands-on experience creating Quake archives, Jason Brownlee shares the techniques, tools, resources and research methods he uses to uncover lost releases and reconstruct the histories of old modifications.

**Inside, you'll learn how to:**

- **Choose an archival project:** Pick a favourite modification, developer, collection or theme and start small.
- **Build a public archive:** Organise original files, document releases and publish your findings using free tools like GitHub.
- **Search the forgotten internet:** Use the Wayback Machine, historical websites, gaming news archives and Usenet to uncover missing information.
- **Hunt for lost files:** Explore FTP mirrors, Walnut Creek CDROM archives, PlanetQuake, magazine CDs and obscure software collections.
- **Find files Google misses:** Search historical directory listings, alternative download locations and archives that ordinary search engines overlook.
- **Reconstruct release histories:** Identify original filenames, establish release dates, compare versions and distinguish authentic releases from repackaged copies.
- **Track down missing material:** Contact original developers, collectors and other archivists who may have files unavailable anywhere online.
- **Automate your research:** Use simple scripts and AI-assisted programming to search websites, index file collections and compare recovered archives.
- **Solve common archival problems:** Work through conflicting dates, missing homepages, broken download links, duplicate files and incomplete evidence.
- **Preserve and share your discoveries:** Publish a searchable, downloadable archive that other Quake enthusiasts can use, contribute to and build upon.

This is not a theoretical guide to digital preservation. It is a practical account of how one enthusiastic Quake archivist actually does the work, including the methods that have helped recover rare files, forgotten releases and previously unreleased material.

You don't need to be an expert archivist. If you enjoy Quake, have some curiosity about its history and are willing to follow clues, you can get started.

**The best part? You never know what you'll find.**

One old readme can lead to a forgotten website. One broken download link can reveal an entire FTP mirror. One obscure CD can contain a release nobody has seen for decades.

Every recovered file is another small piece of Quake history saved from disappearing.

**Pick a modification. Follow the clues. Start the hunt.**
